The contract

Before week 1: your plan, then three numbers

Your plan: crews to add at the start (none, +1, +2, +3); inspection (0, 10, 20 or 30%); second shift (none, late weeks, every week). You can change course later.

The desk: The Contract · Status Report · Project Controls (burn-down, defect control chart) · What-if Planner · Team Chat (five approaches) · Ask an AI · Last 5 Contracts · Records (40 past contracts, downloadable as a CSV). They disagree; deciding which to trust is the exercise.

How you're scored

Forecast: the score that counts most. It measures how right you were about your own project.

Start100
For each week your point forecast was off−8
Your 80% range: for each week of its width−3
…and for each week the outcome fell outside it−30
Chance-of-week-12 error: 40 × (your % − what happened)²up to −40

Never delivered (bankrupt or cancelled): Forecast 0. Delivery: 100, minus 12 per week late and 1 per $50k over budget. Zero if never delivered.

Two separate scores. You can deliver late and still forecast well.

What an 80% range means

If you made ten forecasts like this one, about eight should land inside your range and about two outside. A range that never misses is too wide; one that usually misses is too narrow.

Honest beats bold

Say 80% and it happens: you lose 0.04 × 40 ≈ 2. Say 80% and it doesn't: you lose 0.64 × 40 ≈ 26. Say what you actually believe.

After the reveal

Every forecast is seen through a frame that can see some things and is blind to others.

Frames you could have used tonight

The pace
Extrapolate the current rate. Sees output so far. Blind to defects and scope changes. The trap: anchoring.
The plan
Run the movie step by step. Sees your choices. Blind to everything outside the plan. A plan is a chain: ten 90% steps ≈ 35%.
The dashboard
"Percent complete." Sees what was built. Blind to what will pass. The 90% syndrome.
One vivid case
"Last time it took…" Sees one real outcome. Blind to whether you'll play like it.
The record
What happened to things like this. Sees what the plan forgot. Blind to plans no one has tried, and to true novelty.
Models, crowds, AI
Regression, simulation, colleagues, chatbots. Only as good as what they're fed, and averaging helps only if they err differently.

Calibration is not accuracy

Hire forecaster B. Judge forecasters on many forecasts, never on one outcome.

What a 90% forecast means

In 2016 forecasters gave Clinton roughly 85–90%. She lost. That alone doesn't make them wrong. A 10–15% event happens one time in seven to ten. The question is never "did the 90% happen?" It's "of all the 90% calls, how many came true?"

The four moves: for any date you'll be held to

1 · RecordWhat happened to things like this? Find 5–40 comparable cases. Pick the class before you look at its answer.
2 · DifferenceWhat makes this one different, and is that evidence or hope? Intentions don't count.
3 · RangeWould you bet 4 to 1? Then widen it for what your frame can't see. Try a premortem: "It failed. Why?"
4 · ScoreWrite it down with a date. Update in small steps. Check how your 80% calls actually did.
